An experiment: what an ERP's MCP server is missing before AI writes go everywhere.

I read the published specs for a cannabis ERP's MCP server and wanted to test one idea: as AI assistants start writing to the ERP, three things are missing. A confirm step the ERP controls, an audit log, and a clear label on which actions reach Metrc or QuickBooks. This is a working version of all three, on fictional data. Connect it to Claude and try it.

1. Approval the server controls

Write tools can only request a change. There is no approve tool, so no assistant can approve its own work. A person approves here, with a before-and-after preview.

2. An audit log of every call

Every read, request, approval and applied change is recorded: who, which tool, which systems, and what changed.

3. Labels on what leaves the ERP

Each tool declares whether it reads or writes, and whether it reaches QuickBooks, Metrc or email. Anything reported to the state needs an extra acknowledgement.

What the extra step gets the ERP

A guarantee that holds in every AI clientToday, whether the assistant asks before writing depends on the customer's Claude or ChatGPT settings. A server-side confirm means the answer to "can the AI change my books on its own?" is always no, whichever assistant they use.
Writes can ship soonerWith a human gate on the server, more write tools can go live with less risk, instead of rolling them out one at a time.
An answer for compliance buyersAnything reported to the state is labelled, warned and acknowledged by a named person. That's what a multi-state operator's compliance lead needs before saying yes.
"Who did that?" in one clickThe audit log settles disputes and support tickets: which person, which assistant, which tool, what changed.
Something to charge forReads can stay free. Governed writes, approval rules and audit history are a natural paid or enterprise tier once the beta ends.
Product signalEvery request, approval and rejection shows which writes people trust, which ones they reject, and where it's safe to auto-approve next.

The cost is one extra step per write. Next steps would remove most of it: approval rules (e.g. auto-approve payments under a limit for some roles), batch approval, and approving inside the ERP or in Slack.
